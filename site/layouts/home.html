Why Mero exists

Conservation education should work where the species live.

Indonesia is home to extraordinary biodiversity, yet students living on the frontlines of endangered habitats may not have the connectivity or tools needed to recognize the species around them.

Mero closes that gap with image-based identification and practical conservation education that runs locally on a phone. Recognition becomes a first step toward understanding—and protecting—the surrounding ecosystem.

How identification works

A local reasoning loop, grounded in species data.

The model observes the photograph, searches local candidates, evaluates the evidence, and revises its hypothesis for up to four passes before opening a grounded conversation.

Privacy by architecture

The photograph stays on the phone.

Gemma 4 performs image analysis locally. Mero does not upload photographs, collect personal data, or transmit identification requests to a server.

  • Local image analysis
  • No photo uploads
  • No personal-data collection
  • Fully offline after setup

Current limitations

Know the trade-offs before heading into the field.

  • Large setup: the model requires a one-time download of approximately 2.4 GB.
  • Variable accuracy: rare or visually similar species can still be misidentified.
  • Device-dependent speed: newer hardware completes inference much faster.
  • Battery use: local AI inference is compute-intensive during active identification.
